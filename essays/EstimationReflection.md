# How long does it take you?

## How long did it take you?
Most of my time during this project was spent working on the UI and related functionality. From a technical perspective, writing the code itself was usually not the hardest part for me. Once the direction was clear, implementing UI components and functions progressed relatively smoothly.

What took the most time was the non-coding effort involved in envisioning how users would interact with the interface. In particular, designing visual elements such as graphs and timeline tables required try and error, like avoiding cramping and overflowing form intended areas. One example was creating a timeline-style table that displayed past, ongoing, and future events. Although this initially appeared to be a simple styling problem, it required sympathy of how users would interpret the information and whether the visualization was actually useful.

As a result, a large portion of my effort was spent thinking through user perspective, visual clarity, and usability rather than writing code.

## How long should it take you?
Looking back, the amount of time spent on UI-related tasks makes sense given the importance of user experience. While coding was often straightforward, the process of imagining how users would understand and interact with the UI naturally required more time.

Non-coding effort such as planning, visual design, and empathizing with users should be treated as a core part of development rather than overhead. In future projects, I would intentionally allocate more time to this phase, especially for UI-heavy tasks, since underestimating the thinking and design process can lead to rushed or unclear interfaces.

## From the start to the end
From the beginning of the project, I did not underestimate non-coding effort at all. I believe that ideas do not always appear immediately, and sometimes progress does not happen in linear time. This applies not only to programming, but also to defining goals and justifying design decisions. Spending time thinking without immediate output was often necessary.

At the same time, I learned that overthinking can also prevent progress. Some insights only emerge through actual implementation, and it is often during development that it becomes clear whether the original idea should be followed or pivoted.

My estimation process improved over time. During Milestone 2, I relied mostly on intuition and worked based on how things felt. By Milestone 3, I began using timers and setting personal target times for tasks. This helped me become more aware of how long work actually took and encouraged more focused work within time constraints. As a result, my later estimates were more realistic and reflected actual effort.

## AI Use on Estimation
I did not use any AI tools for effort estimation or effort tracking in this project. All estimates and recorded effort were based on my own work and time tracking.
